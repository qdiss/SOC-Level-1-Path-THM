# SOC Role in Blue Team - TryHackMe SOC Level 1

- **Module:** Blue Team Introduction
- **Date completed:** 2026-09-23
- **Room link:** https://tryhackme.com/room/socroleinblueteam
- **Path:** SOC Level 1

## Overview

This room places the SOC L1 analyst role within the wider company
security structure - who oversees the SOC, what other security
departments exist alongside it, and what a realistic career path looks
like from L1 onward.

## Objectives

- Understand the concept and purpose of the Blue Team
- See where the SOC sits within a company's overall structure
- Learn the SOC L1 analyst career path and next steps

## Tools / Concepts Covered

- Security org structure (CEO → CISO → security departments)
- Red Team / GRC / Blue Team distinction
- SOC roles (L1, L2, Engineer, Manager)
- Cyber Incident Response Team (CIRT/CSIRT/CERT)
- Specialized defensive roles (Digital Forensics, Threat Intel, AppSec, AI Research)
- Internal SOC vs. MSSP (Managed Security Services Provider)

## Analysis Process

### Step 1 - Where the SOC Fits in the Company

The room starts at the top: executives like the CEO focus on business
objectives, not technical security, which is why a CISO (or equivalent
role) is hired to own security strategy and structure the departments
under them. Below the CISO, security typically splits into three
branches: Red Team (offensive, finds issues), GRC (policy and
compliance), and Blue Team (defensive, continuous monitoring and
response) - the branch the SOC belongs to.

### Step 2 - Blue Team Departments Beyond the SOC

Blue Team isn't just the SOC. The room breaks down adjacent
departments: the SOC itself (L1/L2 analysts, engineers, manager) as
the first line of defense; CIRT, brought in when an incident escalates
beyond what the SOC's tooling can handle; and specialized roles
(Digital Forensics, Threat Intelligence, AppSec, AI Research) that
require deep expertise built on top of general SOC/IT experience. This
gave me a clearer map of where L1 sits relative to roles I might grow
into later.

### Step 3 - Career Path and Internal SOC vs. MSSP

The room outlines a realistic path: build core SOC skills, get
practical exposure (CTFs, staying current on threat news, certs like
SAL1), then apply and grow from L1 toward L2 or other specializations.
It also compares working at an internal SOC (fewer tools, steadier
pace) versus an MSSP (many clients/tools, higher pressure, faster
learning curve) - useful context for deciding what kind of first SOC
job to target.

### Final Challenge

The room closes with a scenario-based exercise: acting as a CISO for a
company handling multiple simultaneous incidents, and assigning the
correct security role (SOC, CIRT, Red Team, etc.) to each incident
type based on what was covered in the room.
