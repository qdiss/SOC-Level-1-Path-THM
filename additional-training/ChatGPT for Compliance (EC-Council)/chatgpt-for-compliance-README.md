## Overview

A short course on using ChatGPT responsibly in compliance work: writing
policies and procedures, referencing security standards, reviewing audit
logs, supporting risk assessments and gap analysis, and creating
compliance documentation, while handling the accuracy, privacy, and
regulatory risks of using AI.

## What I Learned

- **AI is a drafting aid, not an authority.** ChatGPT can speed up
  policies, procedures, and reports, but its answers can be inaccurate
  or outdated, so every output needs to be checked against the real
  standard or regulation.
- **Data handling matters.** Sensitive or confidential information
  should not be pasted into a public AI tool, so prompts need to be
  anonymised and organisational rules on AI use must be followed.
- **Structured prompts give better results.** Giving the model a role,
  context, the framework to follow, and the output format produces more
  useful and consistent compliance documents.
- **Compliance tasks it can support:** drafting and reviewing policies,
  mapping controls to frameworks such as ISO 27001, NIST, and GDPR,
  building asset inventories and IAM documentation, summarising audit
  logs, and preparing gap analyses and risk assessments.
- **Limits in a regulatory context:** it does not replace legal advice,
  auditor judgement, or formal evidence, and it cannot be treated as a
  source of record.
- **AI on both sides of security.** The same technology helps
  defenders (for example in threat intelligence and report writing) and
  attackers, which makes responsible use and governance important.

## Reflection

Working through the SOC Level 1 path showed me how much of a
security analyst's job is clear documentation: alert reports using
the Five Ws, escalation notes, and case summaries that another person
can act on without asking questions. Compliance work depends on the
same habit, because policies, risk assessments, and audit evidence only
have value when they are accurate, consistent, and easy to follow.
This course adds the governance side of that picture: why security
controls exist (frameworks and regulations), how they are documented,
and how AI can speed up the writing while a person remains responsible
for checking it. For a SOC role, that helps me understand the
requirements behind the alerts and controls I work with, and it lets
me contribute to documentation and reporting more effectively.
